1. What is a tense?

A tense is the form of a verb that tells us when an action happens and what kind of action it is: finished, going on, repeated, or connected to another time. Tense क्रिया का वह रूप है जिससे पता चलता है कि काम कब हुआ (समय) और किस प्रकार का है (सामान्य, चालू या पूर्ण)।

Two questions decide every tense

  1. Time: is it present, past or future? (काम कब का है?)
  2. Aspect: is the action simple (a fact or habit), continuous (in progress), perfect (completed or linked to another time), or perfect continuous (in progress for a length of time)? (काम किस अवस्था में है?)

3 times × 4 aspects = 12 tenses. Click any box to jump to that tense.

Note on Hindi names. Hindi textbooks use slightly different names for these tenses (for example आसन्न भूत, अपूर्ण भूत, पूर्ण भूत). The English structure is what matters in exams, so this guide gives the closest Hindi meaning and always shows a Hindi example.
A small technical point. Strictly speaking, English verbs change form only for present and past. The future is made with the helper will. School grammar still teaches three times and twelve tenses, and that is the system used here.

2. Verb toolkit: everything you need before the tenses

Tenses are just combinations of a few verb forms and helpers. Learn these once and all 12 tenses become simple.

The five forms of a verb

Helping verbs (auxiliaries): who does what

HelperFormsJob
beam, is, are (present) · was, were (past) · been · beingBuilds every continuous tense (be + V4)
havehave, has (present) · had (past) · havingBuilds every perfect tense (have + V3)
dodo, does (present) · did (past)Makes negatives and questions in simple present and simple past
will / shallwill, shall (would, should in the past)Marks future
The golden rule of English verbs. Only one word in the verb group carries the tense: the first helper. In negatives and questions this tense marker moves to do / does / did, and the main verb goes back to its base form. That is why we say He doesn't go, not He doesn't goes, and Did she write?, not Did she wrote?

Spelling rules

Adding -s / -es (he, she, it)

RuleExamples
Most verbs: add -splay → plays, write → writes, run → runs
Ends in s, sh, ch, x, z, o: add -espass → passes, wash → washes, teach → teaches, fix → fixes, go → goes, do → does
Consonant + y: change y to -iesstudy → studies, carry → carries, cry → cries
Vowel + y: just add -splay → plays, enjoy → enjoys, buy → buys
Specialhave → has

Adding -ing (V4)

RuleExamples
Most verbs: add -ingplay → playing, read → reading, visit → visiting
Ends in silent e: drop the emake → making, write → writing, come → coming
One vowel + one consonant, stressed last syllable: double the consonantrun → running, sit → sitting, stop → stopping, begin → beginning
Ends in ie: change to -yinglie → lying, die → dying, tie → tying
Ends in ee, ye, oe: keep the esee → seeing, dye → dyeing, toe → toeing

Adding -ed (regular V2 and V3)

RuleExamples
Most verbs: add -edwalk → walked, want → wanted
Ends in e: add -dlike → liked, live → lived
Consonant + y: change y to -iedstudy → studied, try → tried
Short verb, one vowel + one consonant: double the consonantstop → stopped, plan → planned

Pronunciation of -ed: /t/ after unvoiced sounds (walked, helped), /d/ after voiced sounds (played, opened), /ɪd/ after t or d (wanted, needed).

Action verbs and stative verbs

Action (dynamic) verbs show an activity: run, write, eat, play. They can be used in every tense. Stative verbs show a state, feeling or opinion, not an activity. They are normally not used in continuous tenses.

TypeCommon stative verbs
Thinkingknow, understand, believe, think (opinion), remember, forget, doubt, realise, mean
Feelinglike, love, hate, want, need, prefer, wish, care
Sensessee, hear, smell, taste, seem, appear, sound, look (seem)
Having & beinghave (possess), own, belong, contain, cost, weigh, be
✗ I am knowing the answer.   ✓ I know the answer.✗ मैं जवाब जान रहा हूँ।   ✓ मैं जवाब जानता हूँ।

Some verbs change meaning and then can be continuous: I have a car (own) but I am having lunch (eat); She thinks it is wrong (opinion) but She is thinking about it (considering); The soup tastes good but He is tasting the soup.

3. Present tenses वर्तमान काल

Present tenses talk about habits, facts, what is happening now, and past actions that are connected to now.

3.1 Simple present

सामान्य वर्तमान काल

Used for habits, facts and fixed schedules.

Structure

PositiveS + V1 (I, you, we, they)  |  S + V5 (s/es) (he, she, it)
NegativeS + do not / does not + V1
QuestionDo / Does + S + V1 ?
I play cricket. / She plays cricket. मैं क्रिकेट खेलता हूँ। / वह क्रिकेट खेलती है।
They do not (don't) play. / He does not (doesn't) play. वे नहीं खेलते। / वह नहीं खेलता।
Do you play? / Does she play? क्या तुम खेलते हो? / क्या वह खेलती है?

Uses

  1. Habits and routines
    My father walks for half an hour every morning. मेरे पिताजी हर सुबह आधा घंटा टहलते हैं।
  2. General truths and scientific facts
    The sun rises in the east. Water boils at 100°C. सूर्य पूर्व में उगता है। पानी 100°C पर उबलता है।
  3. Fixed timetables and schedules (trains, classes, films)
    The school opens at 8 a.m. The train leaves at 6:15. स्कूल सुबह 8 बजे खुलता है। ट्रेन 6:15 पर छूटती है।
  4. Permanent situations and states
    My uncle lives in Jaipur. She works as a teacher. मेरे चाचा जयपुर में रहते हैं। वह शिक्षिका के रूप में काम करती है।
  5. Instructions, directions and recipes
    You turn left, then go straight. आप बाएँ मुड़ते हैं, फिर सीधे जाते हैं।
  6. Commentary, news headlines and story-telling
    Kohli hits the ball and it goes for four! कोहली गेंद मारता है और वह चौका बन जाती है!
  7. Future in time clauses after when, if, before, after, as soon as, unless, until
    I will call you when I reach home. घर पहुँचते ही मैं तुम्हें फ़ोन करूँगा।

Signal words

  • always
  • usually
  • often
  • sometimes
  • rarely
  • seldom
  • never
  • every day / week
  • daily
  • on Sundays
  • once a week
  • generally
Position of frequency words. They go before the main verb but after am/is/are: She always comes on time. / She is always on time.
Common mistake: forgetting -s/-es with he, she, it, or adding it after does. ✗ He go to school. ✓ He goes to school. ✗ Does he goes? ✓ Does he go?

3.2 Present continuous (progressive)

अपूर्ण / तात्कालिक वर्तमान काल

Used for actions in progress now, temporary situations and planned future arrangements.

Structure

PositiveS + am / is / are + V4 (-ing)
NegativeS + am / is / are + not + V4
QuestionAm / Is / Are + S + V4 ?
I am reading. He is reading. They are reading. मैं पढ़ रहा हूँ। वह पढ़ रहा है। वे पढ़ रहे हैं।
She is not (isn't) sleeping. वह सो नहीं रही है।
Are you listening? क्या तुम सुन रहे हो?

Uses

  1. Action happening right now (at the moment of speaking)
    Please be quiet. The baby is sleeping. कृपया शांत रहिए। बच्चा सो रहा है।
  2. Temporary action around now (not necessarily at this exact second)
    I am reading a novel these days. मैं आजकल एक उपन्यास पढ़ रहा हूँ।
  3. Planned future arrangement (with a time, and the plan is already fixed)
    We are visiting Pushkar on Sunday. हम रविवार को पुष्कर जा रहे हैं।
  4. Changing or developing situations
    The weather is getting colder. Prices are rising. मौसम ठंडा होता जा रहा है। दाम बढ़ते जा रहे हैं।
  5. Repeated, annoying habit with always, constantly, forever
    He is always losing his keys! वह हमेशा अपनी चाबियाँ खो देता है! (झुँझलाहट के साथ)

Signal words

  • now
  • right now
  • at the moment
  • currently
  • today
  • these days
  • this week / month
  • Look!
  • Listen!
  • still
Stative verbs (know, like, want, believe, belong) are not used here. Say I want water, not I am wanting water.

3.3 Present perfect

पूर्ण वर्तमान काल (आसन्न भूत)

Used when a past action is connected to the present: by its result, its experience, or because it continues up to now.

Structure

PositiveS + have / has + V3
NegativeS + have / has + not + V3
QuestionHave / Has + S + V3 ?
I have finished my homework. She has finished hers. मैंने अपना गृहकार्य पूरा कर लिया है। उसने अपना पूरा कर लिया है।
They have not (haven't) come yet. वे अभी तक नहीं आए हैं।
Has he eaten? क्या उसने खा लिया है?

Uses

  1. Recent action with a result now (no exact time given)
    I have lost my wallet. (So I don't have it now.) मेरा बटुआ खो गया है। (अभी मेरे पास नहीं है।)
    She has just left. / Have you eaten yet? / I have already paid. वह अभी-अभी निकली है। / क्या तुमने अभी खाया? / मैं पहले ही भुगतान कर चुका हूँ।
  2. Life experience (at any time up to now)
    I have visited Delhi twice. She has never eaten sushi. मैं दो बार दिल्ली जा चुका हूँ। उसने कभी सुशी नहीं खाई।
  3. Action or state that started in the past and continues now (with since / for)
    I have lived here since 2015. He has known her for ten years. मैं 2015 से यहाँ रह रहा हूँ। वह उसे दस साल से जानता है।
  4. Unfinished time period (today, this week, this year)
    I have made three calls today. आज मैंने तीन फ़ोन किए हैं।
  5. Change over time
    Her English has improved a lot. उसकी अंग्रेज़ी में बहुत सुधार हुआ है।

Signal words

  • just
  • already
  • yet
  • ever
  • never
  • so far
  • till now / up to now
  • recently
  • lately
  • since
  • for
  • this week
  • how long
Never use a finished-time word with present perfect. ✗ I have seen him yesterday. ✓ I saw him yesterday. Words like yesterday, last year, in 2019, ago, when (question) belong to the simple past.
have gone vs have been. She has gone to Jaipur = she is there now. She has been to Jaipur = she visited and came back. gone = वहाँ गई हुई है; been = जाकर लौट आई है।
since vs for. since + a point in time (since 2015, since Monday). for + a length of time (for ten years, for two hours).

3.4 Present perfect continuous

पूर्ण अपूर्ण / पूर्ण चालू वर्तमान काल

Used to show how long an action has been going on, or an action that has just stopped and left a visible result.

Structure

PositiveS + have / has + been + V4
NegativeS + have / has + not + been + V4
QuestionHave / Has + S + been + V4 ?
I have been studying for two hours. मैं दो घंटे से पढ़ रहा हूँ।
It has not been raining since morning. सुबह से बारिश नहीं हो रही है।
Have you been waiting long? क्या तुम बहुत देर से इंतज़ार कर रहे हो?

Uses

  1. Action that began in the past and is still going on (duration with since / for)
    She has been teaching English since 2010. वह 2010 से अंग्रेज़ी पढ़ा रही है।
  2. Action that has just stopped, with a visible result
    Your shoes are muddy. Have you been playing in the field? तुम्हारे जूते कीचड़ से भरे हैं। क्या तुम मैदान में खेल रहे थे?
  3. Emphasis on repeated or continuing activity
    He has been calling me all day. वह सारा दिन मुझे फ़ोन करता रहा है।

Signal words

  • for
  • since
  • how long
  • all day / all morning
  • lately
  • recently
Perfect vs perfect continuous. I have read the book (finished, result: I know the story). I have been reading the book (focus on the activity, maybe not finished). Stative verbs (know, have, like) use the plain perfect: I have known him for years.

4. Past tenses भूतकाल

Past tenses talk about finished actions and about the order of events in the past.

4.1 Simple past

सामान्य भूतकाल

Used for finished actions at a definite time in the past.

Structure

PositiveS + V2 (same for all persons)
NegativeS + did not + V1
QuestionDid + S + V1 ?
I watched a film. She wrote a letter. मैंने एक फ़िल्म देखी। उसने एक पत्र लिखा।
They did not (didn't) come. वे नहीं आए।
Did you see him? क्या तुमने उसे देखा?

Uses

  1. Completed action at a specific past time
    We visited the Taj Mahal last year. हम पिछले साल ताजमहल गए थे।
  2. A series of finished actions (a story)
    He opened the door, looked outside and went back in. उसने दरवाज़ा खोला, बाहर देखा और वापस अंदर चला गया।
  3. Past habits or states that are over
    When I was a child, I played in this park every evening. जब मैं बच्चा था, मैं हर शाम इस पार्क में खेलता था।
  4. Past facts in history
    India became independent in 1947. भारत 1947 में स्वतंत्र हुआ।

Signal words

  • yesterday
  • last night / week / year
  • ago
  • in 2019
  • then
  • once
  • the other day
  • when (asking about a past time)
Common mistake: using V2 after did. ✗ She didn't went. ✓ She didn't go. ✗ Did you saw? ✓ Did you see? Once did takes the past marker, the main verb goes back to V1.
Verb “to be” has no did. I was / They were. Negative: I was not. Question: Were you there?

4.2 Past continuous (progressive)

अपूर्ण भूतकाल

Used for an action that was in progress at a moment in the past.

Structure

PositiveS + was / were + V4 (was: I, he, she, it | were: you, we, they)
NegativeS + was / were + not + V4
QuestionWas / Were + S + V4 ?
I was cooking at 7 p.m. They were playing. शाम 7 बजे मैं खाना बना रहा था। वे खेल रहे थे।
She was not (wasn't) listening. वह सुन नहीं रही थी।
Were you sleeping? क्या तुम सो रहे थे?

Uses

  1. Action in progress at a particular past time
    At 9 last night, I was watching TV. कल रात 9 बजे मैं टीवी देख रहा था।
  2. Interrupted action: long action in past continuous, short action in simple past (usually with when)
    I was walking home when it started to rain. मैं घर पैदल जा रहा था कि बारिश शुरू हो गई।
  3. Two actions in progress at the same time (usually with while)
    Mom was cooking while Dad was reading the newspaper. माँ खाना बना रही थीं जबकि पिताजी अख़बार पढ़ रहे थे।
  4. Background description in a story
    The sun was shining and birds were singing when we reached the village. जब हम गाँव पहुँचे, सूरज चमक रहा था और चिड़ियाँ चहक रही थीं।
  5. Repeated annoying past habit with always
    He was always borrowing my pens. वह हमेशा मेरे पेन उधार लेता रहता था।

Signal words

  • while
  • when
  • as
  • at 5 o'clock yesterday
  • all day yesterday
  • at that time
  • from 6 to 7 last evening
when vs while. while + past continuous (the long action). when + simple past (the short action that interrupts).

4.3 Past perfect

पूर्ण भूतकाल

Used for the earlier of two past actions: “the past in the past”.

Structure

PositiveS + had + V3 (same for all persons)
NegativeS + had not + V3
QuestionHad + S + V3 ?
The train had left before I reached. मेरे पहुँचने से पहले ट्रेन जा चुकी थी।
She had not (hadn't) eaten. उसने खाना नहीं खाया था।
Had they finished before the bell rang? क्या घंटी बजने से पहले उन्होंने पूरा कर लिया था?

Uses

  1. Earlier action of two past actions
    When the teacher came, the students had already left. जब शिक्षक आए, छात्र पहले ही जा चुके थे।
  2. In reported speech (present perfect and simple past become past perfect)
    He said, “I have seen it.” → He said that he had seen it. उसने कहा कि उसने उसे देख लिया था।
  3. Unreal past: third conditional and wishes about the past
    If I had studied, I would have passed. / I wish I had listened. अगर मैंने पढ़ाई की होती तो मैं पास हो जाता। / काश मैंने सुना होता।
  4. Cause or background for a past situation
    He was tired because he had worked all night. वह थका हुआ था क्योंकि उसने पूरी रात काम किया था।

Signal words

  • before
  • after
  • already
  • just
  • by the time
  • until
  • when
  • as soon as
  • hardly … when
  • no sooner … than
Common mistake: using simple past for both actions. ✗ When I reached, the train left. ✓ When I reached, the train had left. (The train left first.)
Order words can do the job. If the sentence already has before or after and the order is clear, simple past is also acceptable: After he finished, he left. Use past perfect when the sequence would otherwise be unclear.

4.4 Past perfect continuous

पूर्ण चालू भूतकाल

Used for how long an action had been going on before another past moment or event.

Structure

PositiveS + had been + V4
NegativeS + had not + been + V4
QuestionHad + S + been + V4 ?
I had been waiting for an hour when the bus came. जब बस आई, तब तक मैं एक घंटे से इंतज़ार कर रहा था।
She had not been feeling well. उसकी तबीयत ठीक नहीं चल रही थी।
Had you been working there for long? क्या तुम वहाँ बहुत समय से काम कर रहे थे?

Uses

  1. Duration before a past moment (for / since)
    By 2020, he had been living in Ajmer for ten years. 2020 तक वह दस साल से अजमेर में रह रहा था।
  2. Cause of a past situation (an action that was going on and explains what you saw)
    The ground was wet. It had been raining. ज़मीन गीली थी। बारिश हो रही थी (हो चुकी थी)।

Signal words

  • for
  • since
  • how long
  • before
  • until
  • by the time
  • all day

5. Future tenses भविष्यत् काल

Future tenses talk about actions that have not happened yet. English has no single future ending, so we build the future with will (and shall with I / we).

5.1 Simple future

सामान्य भविष्यत् काल

Used for decisions made now, predictions, promises and offers.

Structure

PositiveS + will / shall + V1
NegativeS + will not (won't) / shall not (shan't) + V1
QuestionWill / Shall + S + V1 ?
I will call you tomorrow. He will come. मैं कल तुम्हें फ़ोन करूँगा। वह आएगा।
They will not (won't) agree. वे सहमत नहीं होंगे।
Will you help me? क्या तुम मेरी मदद करोगे?

Uses

  1. Spontaneous decision (made at the moment of speaking)
    “It's hot in here.” “I will open the window.” “यहाँ गर्मी है।” “मैं खिड़की खोल देता हूँ।”
  2. Prediction based on opinion or belief
    I think India will win the match. मुझे लगता है भारत मैच जीतेगा।
  3. Promise, offer, warning or threat
    I will never lie to you. Shall I carry your bag? मैं तुमसे कभी झूठ नहीं बोलूँगा। क्या मैं आपका बैग उठा लूँ?
  4. Requests and invitations
    Will you close the door, please? क्या आप दरवाज़ा बंद कर देंगे?
  5. Future facts
    Tomorrow will be Monday. I will be 18 next year. कल सोमवार होगा। अगले साल मैं 18 का हो जाऊँगा।

Signal words

  • tomorrow
  • next week / month / year
  • soon
  • tonight
  • in the future
  • one day
  • in 2030
  • I think / I believe / I hope
  • probably
  • perhaps
will vs be going to. Use will for instant decisions and opinion-based predictions. Use be going to for plans made earlier and for predictions with present evidence: Look at those clouds. It is going to rain. More in section 6.
shall. With I / we, shall is used for offers and suggestions (Shall we go?). In modern English will is normal for all persons.

5.2 Future continuous (progressive)

अपूर्ण भविष्यत् काल

Used for an action that will be in progress at a particular time in the future.

Structure

PositiveS + will / shall + be + V4
NegativeS + will not + be + V4
QuestionWill / Shall + S + be + V4 ?
This time tomorrow I will be flying to Mumbai. कल इस समय मैं मुंबई के लिए उड़ान भर रहा होऊँगा।
She will not be attending the party. वह पार्टी में शामिल नहीं हो रही होगी।
Will you be using the car tonight? क्या आज रात तुम गाड़ी इस्तेमाल कर रहे होगे?

Uses

  1. Action in progress at a fixed future time
    At 8 tomorrow morning, I will be teaching Class 10. कल सुबह 8 बजे मैं दसवीं कक्षा को पढ़ा रहा होऊँगा।
  2. Something that will happen in the normal course of events (not a special plan)
    I will be seeing him at the meeting anyway. मैं उससे बैठक में वैसे भी मिल रहा होऊँगा।
  3. Polite questions about someone's plans (softer than “Will you...?”)
    Will you be coming to the function? क्या आप समारोह में आ रहे होंगे?
  4. Two actions going on together in the future
    While I am driving, you will be reading the map. जब मैं गाड़ी चला रहा हूँगा, तुम नक्शा पढ़ रहे होगे।

Signal words

  • this time tomorrow
  • at 5 p.m. tomorrow
  • next Monday at 10
  • all day tomorrow
  • when / while (time clause)
  • in the coming days

5.3 Future perfect

पूर्ण भविष्यत् काल

Used for an action that will be completed before a certain time in the future.

Structure

PositiveS + will / shall + have + V3
NegativeS + will not + have + V3
QuestionWill / Shall + S + have + V3 ?
By 5 p.m. I will have finished the report. शाम 5 बजे तक मैं रिपोर्ट पूरी कर चुका होऊँगा।
They will not have arrived by then. तब तक वे नहीं पहुँचे होंगे।
Will you have completed the syllabus by March? क्या मार्च तक तुम पाठ्यक्रम पूरा कर चुके होगे?

Uses

  1. Action finished before a future deadline
    By next year, she will have graduated. अगले साल तक वह स्नातक हो चुकी होगी।
  2. Assumption about the present or recent past (“I guess it has happened by now”)
    Don't call now. They will have gone to bed. अभी फ़ोन मत करो। वे सो चुके होंगे।

Signal words

  • by
  • by then
  • by the time
  • by next month
  • by tomorrow
  • before
  • by the end of
by vs until. by = not later than (with future perfect: I will have left by 6). until = up to that time (with continuing action: I will stay until 6).

5.4 Future perfect continuous

पूर्ण चालू भविष्यत् काल

Used for how long an action will have been going on up to a point in the future. It is the rarest tense.

Structure

PositiveS + will / shall + have been + V4
NegativeS + will not + have been + V4
QuestionWill / Shall + S + have been + V4 ?
By June, I will have been working here for five years. जून तक मुझे यहाँ काम करते हुए पाँच साल हो चुके होंगे।
He will not have been sleeping for long. वह ज़्यादा देर से सो नहीं रहा होगा।
Will you have been studying for six hours by then? क्या तब तक तुम छह घंटे से पढ़ रहे होगे?

Uses

  1. Duration up to a future point
    Next month we will have been living in this house for ten years. अगले महीने हमें इस घर में रहते हुए दस साल हो जाएँगे।
  2. Cause or explanation of a future situation
    When she arrives, she will have been travelling for 20 hours. जब वह पहुँचेगी, तब तक वह 20 घंटे से सफ़र कर रही होगी।

Signal words

  • by … for
  • by next year
  • by the time
  • for … years / hours
  • how long (by then)

6. Other ways to talk about time

Real English does not always use the “textbook” tense for the time it describes. These are the patterns you need for exams and for real conversation.

Five ways to talk about the future

PatternUseExample
will + V1Instant decision, prediction from opinion, promiseI'll help you.मैं तुम्हारी मदद करूँगा।
am/is/are going to + V1Plan already made; prediction from present evidenceI am going to buy a laptop. / It is going to rain.मैं लैपटॉप खरीदने वाला हूँ। / बारिश होने वाली है।

Time clauses: no “will” after when, if, before, after, as soon as, until, unless

In the time or condition part of a sentence, use the present tense even though the meaning is future.

✗ If it will rain, we will stay home.   ✓ If it rains, we will stay home.अगर बारिश हुई तो हम घर पर रहेंगे।
✗ I will call you when I will reach.   ✓ I will call you when I reach.पहुँचते ही मैं तुम्हें फ़ोन करूँगा।
Present perfect also works here: I will go out after I have finished my work.काम पूरा कर लेने के बाद मैं बाहर जाऊँगा।

used to and would: past habits

FormMeaningExample
used to + V1Past habit or past state that is no longer trueI used to live in Kota. / He used to be shy.मैं कोटा में रहा करता था। / वह शर्मीला हुआ करता था।
would + V1Repeated past action only (not states)Every summer we would visit our grandparents.हर गर्मी हम अपने दादा-दादी के पास जाया करते थे।

Negative and question of used to: I didn't use to smoke. Did you use to play cricket? (after did, it is use to).

7. Sequence of tenses (tense agreement)

When a sentence has two verbs, the tense of the second verb depends on the first.

  1. Main verb in the past → the other verb is also in a past form.
    He said that he was busy.   I thought you would come.उसने कहा कि वह व्यस्त है। / मुझे लगा तुम आओगे।
  2. Exception: universal truths and habits stay in the present.
    The teacher said that the earth moves round the sun.शिक्षक ने कहा कि पृथ्वी सूर्य के चारों ओर घूमती है।
  3. Main verb in the present or future → the other verb can be in any tense that fits the meaning.
    I know that he came yesterday.   She will say that she is ready.मैं जानता हूँ कि वह कल आया था।

Backshift in reported (indirect) speech

When the reporting verb is in the past, the tense of the original words usually moves one step back.

12. Frequently asked questions

How many tenses are there in English?

School and exam grammar teaches 12 tenses (3 times × 4 aspects). Linguists say English has only two tenses by verb form, present and past, because the future is built with the helper will. Follow the 12-tense system for exams.

What is the difference between present perfect and simple past?

Simple past is for a finished time (yesterday, in 2019): I lost my pen yesterday. Present perfect is for an action linked to now with no finished time named: I have lost my pen (and I still don't have it). Simple past में समय बीत चुका होता है; Present perfect में काम का असर अभी भी बना रहता है।

Which tenses are used most in everyday English?

Simple present, simple past, present continuous, present perfect and the will / going to future cover most daily conversation. Master these five first, then add the others.

Why do we say “I am going to Delhi tomorrow” with present continuous?

Present continuous can express a fixed arrangement. The trip is already planned, so English treats it as already in progress. It is more natural than I will go to Delhi tomorrow when the plan exists.

Can I ever use “will” after if?

Not for the future meaning of the condition. But polite requests are an exception: If you will wait here, the doctor will see you shortly (meaning “if you are willing to wait”).

What is the fastest way to learn all tenses?

Learn the helper + verb form pairs from the shortcut table in section 8 (will + V1, be + V4, have + V3, have been + V4). Then write two sentences of your own for each of the 12 tenses using your daily life. Practising with your own examples is far more effective than memorising rules.
