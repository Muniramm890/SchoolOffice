1. What is a preposition?

A preposition is a small word placed before a noun, pronoun or gerund (-ing word) to show its relationship with the rest of the sentence — usually time, place, direction, cause or manner. Preposition वह शब्द है जो किसी संज्ञा या सर्वनाम के पहले लगकर उसका वाक्य के अन्य भागों से संबंध (समय, स्थान, दिशा, कारण आदि) बताता है। इसे हिंदी में "संबंधसूचक अव्यय" कहते हैं।

The pattern behind every preposition

PartRoleExample
PrepositionShows the relationshipThe cat is under the table.बिल्ली मेज़ के नीचे है।
Object of the prepositionThe noun/pronoun/gerund that follows
Prepositional phrasePreposition + object, together

A preposition is never used alone — it always needs an object. And that object is always in its noun form: a noun, a pronoun (in the object form: me, him, her, us, them — never I, he, she), or a gerund (V4, the -ing form), never a plain verb (V1). This single rule solves most preposition mistakes in exams.

Timein, on, at, since, for, by, until… Place / positionin, on, at, above, below, between… Movement / directionto, into, through, across, along… Agent, instrument & mannerby, with, in, on (method) Cause, reason & purposebecause of, due to, for, from Possession, origin & materialof, from, made of/from/with Comparison & contrastlike, as, unlike, despite Fixed combinationsinterested in, afraid of, good at Phrasal / compoundin spite of, according to, instead of
Note on Hindi. Hindi post-positions (में, पर, को, से, के लिए) come after the noun (डिब्बे में). English prepositions come before the noun (in the box). Keep this word-order flip in mind whenever you translate.

2. Preposition toolkit: what you need before the rules

Kinds of prepositions by form

KindWhat it looks likeExamples
SimpleOne short wordin, on, at, by, for, of, to, with, from, up, off
CompoundTwo words fused, often built on a simple prepositioninto, onto, within, without, throughout, upon, inside, outside
Phrasal (complex)A group of words that works as one prepositionin spite of, in front of, because of, according to, instead of, on behalf of
Participle prepositionsAn -ing/-ed word used as a prepositionconsidering, regarding, given, concerning, following, including

What can follow a preposition (the object)

Object typeExample
Nounin the box, on the table
Pronoun (object form)with me, for him, between us — never with I, for he
Gerund (V4)good at swimming, afraid of losing — never afraid of lose
Noun phrase / clausesince the day I met her
The golden rule of prepositions. A verb placed straight after a preposition must be in its -ing (V4) form, never V1. ✗ I am interested in learn English. ✓ I am interested in learning English. ✗ मैं अंग्रेज़ी सीखने में interested हूँ (learn के साथ)। ✓ learning के साथ सही है।

Preposition or something else? Three easy tests

TestHow to checkExample
Followed by a noun/pronoun/gerund?If yes → prepositionsince Monday (prep) vs since he left (conjunction, + clause)
Part of a verb's fixed meaning?If the verb changes meaning with it → phrasal verb particle, not a simple prepositionlook after (take care of) vs sit after him (place)
Comes before a full clause with subject + verb?If yes → it is working as a conjunction, not a prepositionduring the class (prep) vs while the class was on (conjunction)

3. Prepositions of time समय-सूचक

These answer the question when? — at what point, on what day, in what period.

3.1 in / on / at — the time trio

बड़े से छोटे समय की ओर: in (बड़ा) → on (दिन) → at (सटीक समय)

Think of them as zooming in: in for long periods, on for days and dates, at for exact points and fixed expressions.

WordUsed forExamples
inMonths, years, seasons, centuries, long parts of the dayin May, in 2026, in winter, in the 21st century, in the morning/afternoon/eveningमई में, 2026 में, सर्दियों में, सुबह में
onDays, dates, specific named dayson Monday, on 5 June, on my birthday, on Independence Day, on weekends (US)सोमवार को, 5 जून को, मेरे जन्मदिन पर
atExact clock time and fixed time expressionsat 6 o'clock, at noon, at midnight, at night, at the moment, at the weekend (UK), at present6 बजे, दोपहर को, रात को, इस समय
Common mistake: ✗ I get up in 6 o'clock. ✓ I get up at 6 o'clock. ✗ My birthday is at May. ✓ My birthday is in May.
No preposition at all before today, tomorrow, yesterday, tonight, this/next/last + time word, every day: I will call you tomorrow, not on tomorrow.

3.2 since, for, during, by, until, within — time relations

अवधि और सीमा बताने वाले शब्द

Each answers a slightly different time question: starting point, length, when-inside, deadline, or up-to-when.

WordAnswersFollowed byExample
sinceFrom what point?A point in timeI have lived here since 2015.मैं 2015 से यहाँ रह रहा हूँ।
forFor how long?A length/durationI have lived here for ten years.मैं दस साल से यहाँ रह रहा हूँ।
duringAt what point within a period?A named period/event (noun)He fell asleep during the class.वह क्लास के दौरान सो गया।
byNot later than when?A deadline pointSubmit the form by Friday.शुक्रवार तक फ़ॉर्म जमा करें।
until / tillUp to when (continuing)?A point where an action stopsI will wait until 5 o'clock.मैं 5 बजे तक इंतज़ार करूँगा।
withinInside what limit?A time limitThe parcel will arrive within 3 days.पार्सल 3 दिनों के भीतर आ जाएगा।
by vs until, side by side. I will finish by 6 = the action happens once, on or before 6. I will work until 6 = the action continues right up to 6 and then stops.

More time prepositions worth knowing

WordMeaningExample
before / afterEarlier than / later than a pointReach before 9. Call me after lunch.
from … to/tillStart and end of a periodThe shop is open from 9 to 6.
in time (for)Early enough, not lateWe reached in time for the film.
on timeAt the exact planned time, punctualThe train arrived on time.
agoCounted back from now (used after the time, not before)She left an hour ago. (not ago an hour)

4. Prepositions of place स्थान-सूचक

These answer the question where? — the position of one thing relative to another.

4.1 in / on / at — the place trio

अंदर (in), सतह पर (on), बिंदु पर (at)

The same three words work for place too, but the logic changes: enclosed space, touching surface, or a precise point.

WordUsed forExamples
inEnclosed spaces: rooms, buildings, cities, countries, containers, booksin the room, in Jaipur, in India, in the box, in the newspaperकमरे में, जयपुर में, बक्से में
onSurfaces, lines, floors, means of public transporton the table, on the wall, on page 10, on the first floor, on the bus/trainमेज़ पर, दीवार पर, दसवें पन्ने पर
atA precise point or address; events; small/named placesat the door, at 12 MG Road, at the party, at school, at the bus stopदरवाज़े पर, पार्टी में, स्कूल में
in vs at with a car and a bus/train. Small enclosed vehicles use in (in the car, in the taxi, in the auto); vehicles you can walk around in use on (on the bus, on the train, on the plane, on the ship).
Common mistake: ✗ She lives on Jaipur. ✓ She lives in Jaipur. ✗ Meet me in the bus stop. ✓ Meet me at the bus stop.

5. Prepositions of movement & direction गति एवं दिशा-सूचक

These answer the question which way? — used with verbs of motion like go, come, walk, drive, move.

WordMeaningExample
toMovement towards a destinationShe went to the market.वह बाज़ार गई।
towardsIn the direction of (not necessarily reaching it)He walked towards the gate.
intoMovement from outside to insideThe cat jumped into the box.
out ofMovement from inside to outsideHe came out of the room.
ontoMovement to a position on a surfaceThe ball rolled onto the road.
fromStarting point of movement or originThe train arrived from Delhi.
throughFrom one side to the other, inside somethingWe walked through the forest.
acrossFrom one side to the other, on a surface/open areaShe swam across the river.
alongFollowing the length of somethingThey walked along the beach.
overMovement above and across somethingThe cow jumped over the fence.
underMovement passing beneath somethingThe dog crawled under the gate.
up / downMovement to a higher / lower placeShe ran up the stairs and down the hill.
pastMovement beyond a pointHe drove past my house without stopping.
around / roundMovement circling somethingWe drove around the lake.
into vs in, onto vs on. Use into / onto only when there is real movement from one place to another. Use in / on for a fixed position with no movement: He jumped into the pool (movement) but He is in the pool (position).
Common mistake: ✗ She went in the market. ✓ She went to the market. (go in without to sounds like entering a container, not travelling somewhere.)

6. Agent, instrument & manner कर्ता, साधन एवं ढंग-सूचक

These say who did it, with what, and how.

WordMeaningExample
byAgent (the doer) in the passive voice; method of travelThe letter was written by her. We travelled by train.पत्र उसके द्वारा लिखा गया था। हम ट्रेन से यात्रा करते थे।
withInstrument/tool used to do something; accompanimentShe cut the paper with scissors. He came with his friend.उसने कैंची से काग़ज़ काटा।
in (manner)Manner, style, language, ink/pencilWrite in ink. She spoke in Hindi. He replied in anger.
on (method)Method using a device/mediumI heard it on the radio. We spoke on the phone.
by (method, no article)Method of communication, without "the"Send it by email/post. Pay by cheque.
by vs with (the classic pair). by + the doer or the general method (by hand, by bus); with + the specific tool held in hand (with a knife, with a pen). He killed the snake with a stick, and it was done by the guard.

7. Cause, reason & purpose कारण एवं उद्देश्य-सूचक

WordMeaningExample
because ofDirect cause (neutral)The match was cancelled because of rain.बारिश के कारण मैच रद्द हो गया।
due toDirect cause, slightly more formal, often after "be"The delay was due to heavy traffic.
owing toFormal cause, common at the start of a sentenceOwing to the strike, schools remained closed.
forPurpose, benefit, reason for existingThis bag is for books. He was punished for lying.
fromCause of suffering/state (with certain verbs)He is suffering from fever.
for the sake of / on account ofFor the benefit of / because ofHe did it for the sake of his family.
because vs because of. because is a conjunction + full clause (because it rained); because of is a preposition + noun/gerund (because of the rain). Never mix them: ✗ because of it rained.

8. Possession, origin & material अधिकार, उत्पत्ति एवं वस्तु-सूचक

WordMeaningExample
ofPossession, part-whole, quantityThe roof of the house. A cup of tea.
fromOrigin, source, starting pointShe comes from Jaipur. This letter is from my uncle.
made ofMaterial still visible/unchangedThis table is made of wood.
made fromMaterial chemically/physically changed, no longer visible as itselfPaper is made from wood.
made withOne ingredient among severalThis cake is made with butter.
belong toOwnershipThis book belongs to me.
Common mistake: ✗ The chair is made from wood (when the wood is still clearly visible). ✓ The chair is made of wood. Use from only when the original material has been transformed.

9. Comparison & contrast तुलना एवं विरोधाभास-सूचक

WordMeaningExample
likeSimilar to (preposition; + noun/pronoun/gerund)She sings like a nightingale.
asIn the role/function of (not "similar to")He works as a teacher.
unlikeDifferent fromUnlike his brother, he is very shy.
despite / in spite ofAlthough, notwithstanding (+ noun/gerund, no clause)Despite the rain, they played.
compared to / withIn comparisonPrices are high compared to last year.
like vs as. He works like a slave (comparison — he isn't one). He works as a manager (his actual role). like = जैसा (तुलना); as = के रूप में (वास्तविक भूमिका)।

12. Position rules: preposition + gerund, and ending a sentence with a preposition

Rule 1 — After a preposition, the verb must be V4 (-ing)

This is the single most tested preposition rule in Indian school exams.

✗ I am fond of read novels.   ✓ I am fond of reading novels.✗ read के साथ ग़लत। ✓ reading सही है।
✗ Before go to bed, brush your teeth.   ✓ Before going to bed, brush your teeth.
✗ She is good at sing.   ✓ She is good at singing.

Rule 2 — A sentence can end with a preposition

Older grammar books called this wrong; modern English (and most exams) accept it, especially in questions and relative clauses.

Formal: To whom are you speaking?   Natural: Who are you speaking to?दोनों सही हैं; बोलचाल में दूसरा वाक्य ज़्यादा प्रचलित है।
This is the book I was looking for. (perfectly acceptable)

Rule 3 — No preposition before "home", "here", "there" (with motion)

✗ I went to home.   ✓ I went home.   ✗ Come to here.   ✓ Come here.

13. Most confusing prepositions, side by side

These pairs cause the most mistakes because they look similar or overlap in Hindi. Compare them directly.

in / on / at — quick decision guide

Bigger → smaller (time)

in year/month/season → on day/date → at exact hour
in 2026 → on 5 May → at 6 p.m.

Bigger → smaller (place)

in enclosed space → on surface/line → at exact point
in India → on M.I. Road → at shop no. 12
since vs for

since

A fixed point in time — the moment it started.
since 2015, since Monday, since morning

for

A length of time — how much time has passed.
for 10 years, for two hours, for a week
between vs among

between

Exactly two things, or more things thought of one by one.
between the two boys; treaties between countries (each named)

among

Three or more, thought of as a group, not individually.
divided the sweets among the children
beside vs besides

beside

Physical position — next to.
She sat beside me.

besides

Addition — in addition to, apart from.
Besides tea, he had biscuits.
above/over vs below/under

above / over

above = higher, not necessarily directly over. over = directly overhead, or covering/more than.
The fan is above the bed. The bridge is over the river.

below / under

below = lower, general. under = directly beneath, or covered by.
The valley is below the fort. The cat is under the bed.
by vs until/till

by

Not later than — a one-time deadline.
Return the book by Friday.

until / till

Continuing right up to that point.
Keep the book until Friday.
to vs for

to

Destination, recipient, indirect object.
Give this to him. Go to school.

for

Purpose, benefit, duration.
This gift is for you. Wait for an hour.
despite / in spite of vs although / though

despite / in spite of

Preposition — followed by a noun or gerund, never a full clause.
Despite the rain, we played. / Despite being tired, he worked.

although / though

Conjunction — followed by a full clause (subject + verb).
Although it rained, we played.
The one mixing mistake to avoid: ✗ Despite it rained, we played. ✓ Despite the rain, we played. / Although it rained, we played.

14. Twelve common preposition mistakes

WrongRightWhy
I am good on maths.I am good at maths.Fixed adjective + preposition pair.
She is married with a doctor.She is married to a doctor.Fixed adjective + preposition pair.
I am interested on music.I am interested in music.Fixed adjective + preposition pair.
Discuss about the topic.Discuss the topic."Discuss" already means "talk about"; no preposition needed.
He entered into the room.He entered the room."Enter" already means "go into"; no preposition needed with a place.
I reached to the station.I reached the station."Reach" (a place) takes no preposition.
She is afraid from dogs.She is afraid of dogs.Fixed adjective + preposition pair.
He is on home.He is at home.Fixed expression: at home.
I am waiting since two hours.I am waiting for two hours.Since = point; for = duration.
It happened in Monday.It happened on Monday.Days always take "on".
Divide it between the five of them.Divide it among the five of them.More than two → among.
In spite of he was tired, he worked.In spite of being tired, he worked.Despite / in spite of take a noun or gerund, not a clause.

17. Frequently asked questions

How many prepositions are there in English?

There is no fixed number, but around 150 simple and compound prepositions are commonly used, plus many more phrasal prepositions (like in spite of, according to). School grammar usually focuses on about 30–40 that appear most often.

What is the easiest way to remember in / on / at?

Think of them as zooming from big to small: in for the biggest unit (year, country), on for the middle unit (day, surface), at for the smallest, most exact point (clock time, an address). बड़े से छोटे की ओर सोचें: in → on → at.

Why can a sentence end with a preposition?

English naturally moves the preposition to the end in questions and relative clauses (Who did you go with?). This was once considered incorrect in formal writing, but it is now accepted in both spoken and most written English.

Is "discuss about" correct?

No. Discuss already means "to talk about", so it takes a direct object with no preposition: Let's discuss the plan, not discuss about the plan. The same is true for enter (a place) and reach (a place).

Why is it "afraid of" but "married to"?

Most adjective-preposition pairs are fixed by convention rather than logic, exactly like vocabulary. The only reliable way to get them right is to learn each pair as a small phrase, not word by word — the collocation tables in section 10 are a good starting list.

What is the fastest way to master prepositions?

Learn the three groups in this order: (1) the in/on/at rules for time and place, since they cover most daily sentences; (2) the confusing pairs in section 13, since they cause most mistakes; (3) ten new verb/adjective + preposition collocations a week from your own reading. Testing yourself with the practice section above is more effective than just re-reading the rules.
